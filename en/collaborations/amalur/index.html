International conference: dialogue between Navarre and Argentina

28–30 October 2026 · Pamplona

Child abduction and enforced disappearance under dictatorial regimes

The Public University of Navarre and the Parliament of Navarre organised this conference to bring the Spanish and Argentine experiences into dialogue on child abduction, the restitution of identity and victims’ rights.

Over three days, the event brought together historical research, legal analysis, institutional experience and personal testimonies. The programme addressed repressive institutions, legal responses and policies on truth, justice, memory and reparation.

The conference was directed and coordinated by Mikel Lizarraga Rada, a lecturer in the History of Law and Institutions at the Public University of Navarre. Its purpose was to connect academic knowledge with the institutions responsible for making victims’ rights effective, with the participation of those directly affected.

The victims’ testimony panel

On Friday 30 October, at the Parliament of Navarre, the testimony panel formed the fourth and final part of the conference.

Following the historical, legal and institutional sessions, this panel brought in the experiences of people who had been taken as children and of families who continue searching. It provided a space for them to speak about the consequences of what happened, the processes of searching and recovering identity, and the need for recognition, justice and reparation.

The panel was moderated by Amaia Otazu, a journalist with Radio Nacional de España, and brought together the testimonies of:

  • Fanny Erro, representing AMALUR — Asociación de Bebés Robados de Navarra, a mother searching for her first daughter, who was taken after birth.
  • Beatriz Mahave Salcedo, also representing AMALUR, an adopted person searching for her original family.
  • Soledad Luque Delgado, president of “Todos los niños robados son también mis niños”, who is searching for her twin brother Francisco.
  • Manuel Gonçalves Granada, a victim of child appropriation during the Argentine dictatorship, secretary of Abuelas de Plaza de Mayo and a representative of CoNaDI.
  • Olmo Gómez Aldaz, a victim of abduction and illegal adoption whose judicial recovery of his original parentage is directly connected to Navarre.

Olmo Gómez Aldaz’s participation

After learning about the conference, Olmo Gómez Aldaz located its director, Mikel Lizarraga Rada, and contacted him to share his story. It was through that contact that Mikel first learned of his case, which sparked considerable interest. After speaking by telephone, he invited Olmo to take part in the victims’ testimony panel.

His experience has a direct family and judicial connection to Navarre. His original family is from Navarre, and the courts in Navarre recognised his original paternal parentage through a judgment of Court of First Instance No. 9 in Pamplona, upheld by the Provincial Court of Navarre on 12 May 2025.

The recovery of his original parentage and surnames while retaining his adoptive parentage, together with his official recognition as a victim of Francoism, brought a concrete experience to the conference of both the progress achieved and the questions of reparation that remain unresolved.

His participation also coincided with his joining AMALUR.
